Hi Pawan -

I'm open from 8-8:45, 9:30-11, and 1:30-3.  But I am not sure how I can help with finalizing your chemicals.  Rafael should work with Michelle and the PROM committee.  I haven't kept up with the exchanges between Rafael and the PROM. But from the last email that I replied to, I think it's less of an issue of the actual chemicals themselves than detailing the protocols.

We have fume hoods and glove boxes which are basically for non-chemists to perform basic chemistry.  We are not a chemistry lab, so don't are not set up with full protocols nor do our students have the full experience and training of chemists.  We thus ask for your forbearance in working with us on your experimental requirements.

You will need to provide protocols -- less about "how to" than to ensure due diligence with regard to safety.  There was a serious accident some ten years ago when an engineering post-doc was doing some serious chemical synthesis.  As you know, engineering is not chemistry.  He was seriously injured.  Life threatening injuries.  I saw the hood.  The blast shields were gone and there was a basketball sized hole in the fume hood deck so you could see the chemical storage underneath.  Rumor has it that he was following a standard protocol for the lab he had done many time before -- but scaling up some 10,000X.  My husband, a former synthetic chemist, tells me there are protocols for scaling up, but an engineer is not likely to think about them.  I know this lab and I know the people who used and ran it -- they are careful and conscientious.  But it was not a synthesis lab.  So, I have a great deal of respect for chemistry and chemists -- and the fact that no one who uses our 155 lab is a chemist - including Michelle and I, who are actually chemical engineers, a totally different thing.  Therefore, we will not allow chemistry until due diligence has been demonstrated and documented.

So, the protocols you will have provided should have identities and volumes of all the chemicals used, any heating or mixing done, provisions for storage and waste, etc.  In addition, the time and space required for your experiments -- ideally, everything should be cleaned up and stowed away so the next person can use the station.  Some of this, you will have to work with Michelle to learn how things are currently done.  But for much of this, I think that the standard best practices for safety review of a chemistry protocol should be sufficient.

So, if you'd like to bring your protocols, I'd be glad to do an initial review.  And, we can talk about your list of chemicals and how to manage them.

Mary

We have fume hoods and glove boxes which are basically for non-chemists to perform basic chemistry.  We are not a chemistry lab, so don't are not set up with full protocols nor do our students have the full experience and training of chemists.  We thus ask for your forbearance in working with us on your experimental requirements.

You will need to provide protocols -- less about "how to" than to ensure due diligence with regard to safety.  There was a serious accident some ten years ago when an engineering post-doc was doing some serious chemical synthesis.  As you know, engineering is not chemistry.  He was seriously injured.  Life threatening injuries.  I saw the hood.  The blast shields were gone and there was a basketball sized hole in the fume hood deck so you could see the chemical storage underneath.  Rumor has it that he was following a standard protocol for the lab he had done many time before -- but scaling up some 10,000X.  My husband, a former synthetic chemist, tells me there are protocols for scaling up, but an engineer is not likely to think about them.  I know this lab and I know the people who used and ran it -- they are careful and conscientious.  But it was not a synthesis lab.  So, I have a great deal of respect for chemistry and chemists -- and the fact that no one who uses our 155 lab is a chemist - including Michelle and I, who are actually chemical engineers, a totally different thing.  Therefore, we will not allow chemistry until due diligence has been demonstrated and documented.

So, the protocols you will have provided should have identities and volumes of all the chemicals used, any heating or mixing done, provisions for storage and waste, etc.  In addition, the time and space required for your experiments -- ideally, everything should be cleaned up and stowed away so the next person can use the station.  Some of this, you will have to work with Michelle to learn how things are currently done.  But for much of this, I think that the standard best practices for safety review of a chemistry protocol should be sufficient.

So, if you'd like to bring your protocols, I'd be glad to do an initial review.  And, we can talk about your list of chemicals and how to manage them.

but it's up to you as to how to make your project.? Rather like learning 
how to use the tools in a machine shop to make your own custom 
motorcycle.? We can show you how to make the parts, but not how to put 
them together.

We're a lab for tinkerers.? Now, I know your next question:? can you 
hook me up with some of your tinkerers?? Well, we do have a list of 
independent consultants -- they are not affiliated with Stanford, but 
are qualified labmembers -- and you are welcome to contact them (there's 
a list on our website.)? However, I wouldn't recommend this unless there 
is expertise on OLED fabrication in your startup.? Like the motorcycle, 
the consultants can make your parts but no guarantee they will all fit 
together into the ride you envisioned.

I don't mean to be discouraging, but rather, set some expectations 
before we get too far into the process.? I'd be glad to chat if you'd
