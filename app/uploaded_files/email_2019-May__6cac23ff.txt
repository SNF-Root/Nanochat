Hi Xiaoqing, et al -


First, for reference, here's the 
https://suwater.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj7756/f/wastewater_bmp_factsheet.pdf


I'd like to propose the following as a general approach for situations 
like this.? I think it would be OK to aspirate for this experiment -- 
and better for safety reasons.? Here's what I'm thinking (and please 
provide counterarguments as you see them!)


1.? Aqua Regia (HCl, Nitric acid), when diluted and neutralized, is 
acceptable AWN waste.


2.? Platinum is not listed on this sheet with a discharge limit. That's 
not to say that just because it doesn't appear on the sheet it's OK to 
discharge -- BUT this sheet shows all the common industrial wastes of 
concern -- noble metals are common in industrial use - and generally not 
considered hazardous.? AND, it would be good to double-check, but I 
imagine that the amount Archana plans to etch will be a thin layer that 
will result in concentrations (etchant plus 3X rinses) that will be 
nowhere near the mg/L scales on this sheet.


3.? Safety.? When I hear about chemical incidents in other labs like 
ours, it's invariably because they don't have a functional AWN (in one 
case, they had one, but decided not to commission it for reasons of 
cost) and they had aqua regia in a container without pressure relief or 
had inadvertently come into contact with combustible materials.? So, I 
would much rather use our AWN in those situations for aqua regia (and 
other mixes) where the identity and concentration of waste materials is 
not incompatible with the wastewater policy.


What do you all think?


Mary

DISPOSAL

Pour excess and waste aqua regia into a large quantity of ice (500 grams of ice per 100 mL of aqua regia).
