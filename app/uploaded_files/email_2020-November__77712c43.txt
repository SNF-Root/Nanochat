Cool!? I think it would help to know what "films" means....? The 
protocol seems to be for synthesis of perovskite nanoparticles - does 
this mean that the films are nanoparticle-based?

My two cents'....

In general, we've treated silicon samples that have nanoparticle 
solutions drop-cast and dried on them as pretty inert, on the assumption 
that the film adheres really well.? We've also treated silicon samples 
coated with 2D and HgTe material films as pretty inert.? For example, 
we've routinely allowed HgTe and lead-based films on silicon on the 
Headway, for resist coating, the Raith for patterning, and dedicated 
beakers in the wet station for developing. These processes don't affect 
the films.?? (It's when people need to etch them that we start to 
worry.? In fact, the HgTe people use the ion mill to etch their patterns 
because we had concerns.)

So...? if these films are strongly adherent to an inert substrate, I 
think it should be OK. We might want to take extra measures to ensure 
the substrates don't contact the holder or anything else that might 
touch other people's samples.

If the films are not adhered to anything, then any of the above doesn't 
apply.

> In general, we've treated silicon samples that have nanoparticle solutions
> drop-cast and dried on them as pretty inert, on the assumption that the

> 2D and HgTe material films as pretty inert.  For example, we've routinely
> allowed HgTe and lead-based films on silicon on the Headway, for resist

1) The PEDOT:PSS solution will be spincoated using headway on quartz chip and heated.

4) An ebeam lithography (with Raith at SNSF) process will be run to pattern the ebeam resist.
5) The PEDOT:PSS will be etched using PT-OX at SNF with the ebeam resist masks.

Hiya -

Sorry, Phil, can I weigh in?

1.  Mixing.  Since it's crystalline powder, it needs to be mixed outside of the cleanroom.  The wafersaw room is fine, except that the lights need to be off and windows taped.  If they bring a bug lamp, they should be able to see enough to work without exposing resist.  I think we have one, but can't tell you exactly where it is right now (155?)  If they use, then we should have a method where the other systems in that room are reserved or blocked from being used.

2.  Spinning.  I'm not inclined to have this spun up in the cleanroom because the aerosol from the offspray during the spin will dry and turn into fine particles.  This is mostly contained in the bench, but will be all over the bench.  We have had reports from students about nanoparticle contamination on their samples from the headway -- I'm not sure it was ever conclusive, but circumstantial evidence was reasonable (SEM's of perfectly round, perfectly uniform-sized particles.)  So, what we've done in the past is to allow polymer-based microspheres (that can be cleaned up with acetone), but not metal nanoparticle solutions in the cleanroom coaters.  There is a spin coater in the wafersaw room -- I think it may be badgerized -- that they can use.

3. Storing.  I'd prefer that the crystals and the resist-containing crystals not be stored in the cleanroom.

4.  Waste.  Cobalt is one of the heavy metals that is banned from the waste stream.  I realize amounts are small, but the right thing to do would be to collect all liquid waste (like used developer solution) into properly labeled containers.

Mary
