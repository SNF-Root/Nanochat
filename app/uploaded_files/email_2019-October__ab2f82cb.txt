Hello Kevin,

Your project sounds very doable from a technical perspective. We do allow
Kapton tape into our equipment, but I should warn you that it outgases
during the deposition, preventing nucleation in an area around the tape and
I would imagine being incorporated into the film that does get deposited.
Depending on what you plan to do with the Al2O3, that may be an issue.

Logistically speaking, SNF staff does not provide processing services. If
you would like to have someone else deposit the film (you are always
welcome to come and join the lab and run the processes yourself!), you can
see if one of the consultants might be able to take on your project. The
consultants are not Stanford employees, so your discussions about fees will
need to be conducted with them specifically, but yes, you will also be
charged for equipment time, which will be in part related to the
thicknesses and temperatures you plan to run. We do not have a monthly fee
associated with setting up accounts or using our labs.

Here is a link to the consultants information:
https://snfexfab.stanford.edu/snf/contacts/outside-consultants

Please let me know if you have further questions!

Best,
Michelle

Hi Gaurav -

I'm afraid your PROM form needs a bit more work.? Please understand that 
we do not have experience with these materials either in deposition or 
in etching.? In general, there are a number of safety concerns with new 
materials -- some of these in particular (such as the high reactivity of 
elemental strontium, high toxicity of some ruthenium oxides, etc.) And 
even while users might not be personally exposed to these materials, we 
also have to consider our staff who have to clean chambers, install 
sources, and change pump oil on equipment that has been used to process 
these materials.? Also, other users may be concerned about potential 
cross-contamination of samples processed in equipment following your 
materials.? So, your help in bringing us up to speed on these materials 
would be much appreciated -- it would enable us to manage safety as well 
as minimize potential impact to others' work.? It would enable us to set 
up systems to support your work.

You may have already spoken with Carsen and Maurice about these 
materials, but the purpose of the PROM request is to ensure due 
diligence by documenting these discussions.

In this vein, it would help to know:

1.? How much material are we talking about?? It's a big difference 
between a cassette of 25 wafers every week, for example, versus a few 
pieces every now and then.? And generally, how thick is the material?

2.? How are the materials deposited?? Are they sputtered from a special 
target or reactively deposited?? Will you be providing your own targets?

3.? What, if any, precautions are taken in your lab/your supplier's lab 
to ensure safety and contain potential contamination concerns?? In 
particular, are there special precautions for handling targets, cleaning 
chambers, and taking care of pumps?

4.? What are the vapor pressures of your deposited materials?

I expect that we will have more questions, but this is a good start.? 
Again, any info and insight you have on best practices that will help us 
to manage these materials to protect our staff, our users, and their 
work, would be much appreciated.

If you would like to see examples of other PROM forms, check out the 
archive at: 
https://snfexfab.stanford.edu/snf/lab-user-guide/materialschemicals/process-or-materials-review-requests-prom-and-form

Feel free to discuss with any of us on the committee (basically, most 
all of the Process staff plus Carsen and Graham.)

Thanks,

Mary

protocol for using EKC-265. Will this one work for you? If so, could you

I will be using wbflexcorr3 and 4 for this process:

?      Reserve both wbflexcorr 3 and 4. Activate wbflexcorr 3. (While
working with EKC265, other people can not work aside)

?      Obtain and label a waste container.

?      Fill blue ?in-use hazardous chemical? tags.

?      Fill a beaker with EKC265, and place a thermometer into the beaker.

?      Slowly heat up this beaker on the hotplate to 65 ~75 degree C.
(Monitor the temperature with the thermometer, and stir the liquid
frequently.)

?      Load the sapphire wafers into a cassette.

?      Dip the cassette into the heated EKC265 solution. Let the samples
soak in EKC265 for 30min.

?      Prepare three beakers of DI water.

?      Dip the cassette up and down inside the first DI water 5 times

?      Repeat last step with the other 2 DI beakers.

?      Place the last DI beaker under running DI water, and let it overflow
for 5 min.

?      Dry the samples with N2 gun.

?      Collect EKC265 waste with the waste bottle. Fully rinse all beakers,
thermometer, and the cassette, Collect the rinsed DI water with the waste
bottle if it is colored.

?      Place the waste bottle in room 162A.
